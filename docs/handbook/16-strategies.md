# 16. Strategies: How to Study Well with the Program

This chapter arranges the tools from the previous chapters into ways of working. These are suggestions, not rules; the text itself determines the path.

## 16.1 The Basic Path: Observe – Interpret – Apply

**1. Observe: What does it say?**
Read the passage several times, in two translations side by side (Parallel, [Chapter 2](02-reading.md)). Highlight repetitions, connecting words ("for", "therefore"), people, places, and times. The *Section study* template gives you the three headings.

**2. Interpret: What does it mean?**
- Interpret the passage in its **context**: What comes before and then after?
- Look up the most important words (Word study, [Chapter 4](04-original-text-word-study.md)).
- Look at the **Cross-references**: Where does Scripture explain itself?
- Clear passages interpret unclear ones, never the other way around.

**3. Apply: What does this mean for me?**
Write one sentence in a note or a to-do ("This week ..."). Without application, study remains knowledge without fruit.

## 16.2 A Word Study

1. Click a word in the text (Word study on) and note the Strong's number.
2. Look at **All occurrences** and sort them by book: Where does it appear most often?
3. Look at the **renderings**: How is the same original word translated elsewhere?
4. Read the basic meaning in the Dictionary (BDB, LSJ).
5. Go back to the starting passage: Which meaning fits here? The **context** decides, not the dictionary.
6. Record the result in a document using the *Word study* template.

**Warning:** Meanings are not additive. In any one passage, a word usually has **one** meaning, not all of those the dictionary lists.

## 16.3 A Topic Study

1. Collect the most important terms of the topic as Strong's numbers.
2. Search with several terms (`G26 G5485`) or with proximity ("in the same chapter").
3. In the **Topic network** and **Word network**, look for neighbors you hadn't thought of.
4. Save the hits **as a document** and organize them there.
5. Check the Arc of the Bible: How does the topic develop from the OT to the NT (graph, OT ↔ NT)?

## 16.4 A Person Study

1. Open the **Family tree**: origin, family.
2. **People network**: Who appears together with this person?
3. **Map** with travel route: Where did their path lead?
4. Fill in the *Person study* template. The questions: Who is this person? Family and origin. Character and relationship with God. Key passages. What do I learn from this?

## 16.5 Studying a Book

- First read the **whole book** in one sitting (a reading plan helps).
- Then do a Section study, chapter by chapter.
- Keep **one overview document** per book with `[[Links]]` to the individual studies. The Knowledge graph will show you the network later.
- Highlight a book's key words in a fixed color; the Highlights collect in the collection.

## 16.6 Preparing a Sermon or Bible Study

See [Chapter 8](08-sermon.md). Key points: central message in **one** sentence, outline drawn from the text, time for each part, handout.

## 16.7 Memorizing

- **Short passages**: better 2 verses learned securely than 10 half-learned.
- **5 minutes daily** instead of a lot at once.
- First **read the verse aloud**, then practice with gaps. The gaps grow as your ability does.
- Tie new verses to fixed points in your day (breakfast, commute).
- Old verses stay in the system; the program brings them back in time.

## 16.8 Learning Greek and Hebrew

- Start with the **alphabet** (a few days), then the **Letter quiz**, then the **vocabulary trainer**.
- Learn the **most frequent words** first; the trainer sorts by frequency.
- Apply what you've learned right away: in the Word study you'll recognize the words again.
- Use the grammar search to see a pattern (for example aorist) across many verses.

## 16.9 Group Study

- Everyone reads the same passage beforehand and brings **three observations**.
- One person shows the text in the program (Parallel, Study together, [Chapter 10](10-studying-together.md)).
- The group writes a central message together.
- One to-do per person: a concrete application by the next meeting.

## 16.10 A Study Session in 20 Minutes

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Open the plan, read the chapter of the day |
| 3–8 | Look up two words or view cross-references |
| 8–12 | A note with observation and application |
| 12–15 | Memorization practice |
| 15–20 | Prayer list: add a request, review answered ones |

## 16.11 Principles the Program Supports

- **Scripture alone:** The Bible text first, commentaries only afterward (and shown only on request).
- **Context before single verse.**
- **Test instead of believe:** Check AI answers and word meanings against the text too.
- **Record your own work:** What isn't written down is lost.
