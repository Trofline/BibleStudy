# 5. Notes, Highlights and Bookmarks

Everything you write or mark yourself belongs to the **active vault** (your folder, see [Chapter 15](15-backup-privacy.md)). This chapter explains the three simplest tools.

## 5.1 The differences at a glance

| Tool | Purpose | Has a note? | Has a color? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Note** | a thought about a verse | yes | no |
| **Highlight** | emphasize a word or verse in color | optional | yes, with folders and tags |
| **Bookmark** | mark a passage so you can jump back to it | no | no (just a star ★) |

## 5.2 Notes

1. Click a verse.
2. Right sidebar → **Notes** (or right-click → *Write note*).
3. Write the note. It is saved automatically.

Verses with a note show a ✎ next to the verse number. Below, in the tab, you'll find all your notes; clicking one jumps to the passage.

### Voice note on a verse

In the verse menu (right-click, long press on a phone) you record a voice note with the **microphone** (computer, Android and iPhone; up to 5 minutes). Then you listen to it, give it a name and keep or discard it. A **♪** stands next to the verse number; playing and deleting work in the verse menu and in the verse overview. Voice notes are kept in the vault and therefore **synchronise**.

## 5.3 Highlights

There are three kinds:

- **Word or piece of text:** drag with the mouse, also across several verses (from the middle of verse 1 to the middle of verse 2). The colors appear right above the text (can be turned off). Double-click selects a word, triple-click the verse. The selection snaps to **whole words** (can be turned off), and the verse number cannot be marked. Blanks at the start and end are cut off.
- **Whole verse:** click the **verse number**; the colors appear over the verse. **Ctrl+click** chooses single verses, **Shift+click** a range of whole verses. On a **phone** you hold your finger on the verse number and move it down (or up): every verse up to there is chosen, and when you lift your finger the colors appear for all of them.
- **Sub-highlight:** inside an already highlighted verse you mark the most important part. There is only one option there, **Sub-highlight**, in the accent color or a color of your choice (Settings, own colors too); **…** gives a normal highlight instead. In the collection it is shown highlighted inside the verse, not as an entry of its own.

A **✕** on the far left above the text always removes the highlight. After a click on the number of a highlighted verse it removes the whole highlight, also when it spans several verses. A **plain click** on highlighted words (no dragging) opens the colors for that highlight so you can recolor or delete it without marking the words again. A click on the verse number of a highlighted verse shows its current color.

With **Quick** (switch in the bar) whatever you select is highlighted at once in the quick color; a click on the verse number highlights the whole verse, another click removes it. **Ctrl+Z** undoes the last highlights.

A highlight over several verses stays **one** entry (“4–6”). Highlights also show in other translations (whole verses always, single words when the wording or the Strong numbers match; can be turned off). You choose a color; you set the default color in the Settings. A highlight can carry its own **note**.

### Own headings

In the verse menu (right-click) you can put an **own heading** before a verse (not a note). It shows in the reading view, in search and in the Obsidian export.

### Folders, tags, categories

Under *Notes → Highlights* (the collection) you organize highlights into **folders** and give them **tags**. The button at the top right shows the highlights of **all vaults** at once (also in the reading text, to look at only) or only those of the active one; the state is kept. If you like, the program assigns the **book name as a tag** when saving (setting). Clicking an entry jumps to the passage.

### Sort by colour

In the **Marks** tab the button with the **palette** sorts the list by **colour** instead of by folder: all yellow verses together, all green ones, and so on, whatever folder they are in. The heading of a colour carries its name (see *name colours*) or the colour value; a click folds the group. The button remembers its state. The filters (folder, tags) and the *all vaults* view still apply.

### The Explorer

In the **Explorer** tab (right sidebar) you see all highlights and notes like in a file explorer: with folders and sub-folders, a tree on the left, the path on top and a sortable list (click a column heading).

- **Arranging:** put items and folders into a folder by dragging, or with **Ctrl+X** / **Ctrl+V**. **Double-click** or **Enter** opens, **Del** deletes, **Ctrl+Z** undoes deleting, moving and renaming. Arrow keys select, with Shift several, **Ctrl+A** everything.
- **Typing jumps:** type “u” and you land on the first entry starting with “u”. Typing on narrows it down. The field above filters the folder.
- **Several at once:** select, then right-click: **Change color …** and **Tags** (add or remove) for all selected highlights. This can be taken back as well.
- **Select as in a photo gallery:** the **Select** button (on a phone also a long press on an entry) turns every row into a box to tick. A tap ticks or unticks it; nothing opens. A bar below shows how many are chosen and does something with all of them: **All**, **Move**, **Reading path** (in the order you tapped them), **More …** (color, tags, other vault) and **Delete**. **Done** ends selecting.
- **Trash:** what you delete goes to the **trash** (bottom of the tree). It stays there for **30 days**, also after a restart. **Double-click**, **Enter** or right-click → *Restore* brings it back; a deleted folder returns with its content. *Delete for good …* and *Empty trash …* ask first. Documents and highlights removed in the reading text land there too.
- **Trash button:** above the list of documents and in the bar that appears after deleting, a button opens the trash with everything that lies there.
- **Copy or move into another vault:** marks and notes can be copied or moved into another vault, with colour, note, tags and their folder. Long folder names end with "…" in the tree and the path.
- **Undo in the reading text too:** when a highlight is removed in the text or in the *Highlights* tab, **Undo** appears briefly at the bottom.
- **Exporting:** right-click a folder → as PDF / print or as a Markdown file.
- **Linking notes:** Bible references in a note (for example John 3:16) become links below the note. The note on John 3:16 shows at the bottom which other notes refer to it.

### Reading paths

A **reading path** links verses (highlighted or not) in the order in which they build on each other, for example “Justification”: Rom 3:23 → Rom 6:23 → Rom 5:8 → Eph 2:8–9.

- **Create:** right-click a verse (on a phone: long press) → **Reading path** → *New reading path from here …*. Add more verses with *Add to “…”*. When several verses are chosen, all of them go in. In the Explorer, **Select** and **Reading path** make a path from the chosen highlights.
- **In the text:** in the left margin every step shows its **number** in the color of the path. From step to step in the same chapter an **arrow** runs along the margin, like the jumps beside the code in a disassembler (such as IDA). Each arrow has its own lane so nothing overlaps; an arrow going up (back) is dashed.
- **Read on:** below every step a small bar shows the step before, the name and the number (e.g. “2/5”) and the next step, also in another chapter or book. A click goes there.
- **Edit:** *Reading path → Edit reading paths …* (or a click on the number in the margin, or on the name below the verse): move steps up and down, take them out, rename, change the color, delete. You can also switch off the arrows in the margin there.
- **Add quickly:** at the very top of the verse menu stands *＋ Add to “…”*: it adds the verse (or the chosen verses) to the reading path you used last. If you chose verses on the phone by their number, the same button sits in the color bar above the verses.
- **In the Explorer:** a reading path can belong to a **folder**: right-click the folder (or the free space of the list) → **Reading paths** and tick the path, or click **Folder …** in the window *Edit reading paths*. *New reading path from this folder …* makes a path of all entries of the folder, in the order of the Bible. When you open the folder, the path stands above the list with **▶ Read** (to the first step) and **Arrange …**, and in the left margin arrows lead from entry to entry in the order of the path. When you change the order of the steps or sort the list differently, the arrows are drawn anew. Right-click an entry → **Reading path** moves it earlier or later, takes it out or adds it to a path. Renaming or moving the folder keeps the path with it.
- **Share:** in the window *Edit reading paths*, **Export …** saves one path or all of them as a file (`.bspath`), if you like **with the marks** on these verses (exactly which passages, in which color). **Import …** (also in the verse menu under *Reading path* and in the Explorer) reads such a file. You decide:
  - **Keep** (the path goes into your vault) or **Only look** (it lasts until you close the program; **Keep** in the window *Edit reading paths* saves it after all).
  - If the file brings marks: **Tint only** (the verses show in their colors, nothing is saved, your own marks stay untouched), **Take over and save** (the marks go into your vault; what is there already does not come twice) or **Without marks**.

Reading paths belong to the vault; they are backed up and synchronised (also which folder they belong to).

## 5.4 Bookmarks

Bookmarks are the simplest list: verses you want to jump back to later, without a note.

- **Set:** Click a verse, then **Bookmark** at the top or **Ctrl+D**. Pressing it once more removes it. Also in the verse menu: *Bookmark / remove bookmark*.
- **Star:** A bookmarked verse shows a ★ next to the verse number.
- **List:** Right sidebar → **Bookmarks**. Clicking an entry jumps to the passage; the ✕ removes the bookmark.

In older versions, bookmarks were called "Merkliste". They now have their own group in the sidebar.

> **Which one when?** If you just want to find again what you're currently reading: bookmark. If you want to capture something: note. If you want to emphasize a word or sort things: highlight. If the verse should become a task: to-do (see [Chapter 7](07-planning.md)).

## 5.5 Where do I find everything I've written about a verse?

In the **Verse overview** (Ctrl+G). It shows your notes, documents and highlights for that verse. With **Ctrl+K** you can also find all your own material (documents, notes, highlights with their notes and tags, prayers, to-dos, your own cross-references) through a single field.

## 5.6 Backing up and taking things with you

Everything lives in the vault and is included in every backup. For sharing:

- *Settings → Export …* writes the active vault, including settings, as a ZIP; *Import …* creates a **new** vault from it.
- *Export as Obsidian vault …* (also by right-click in the Explorer) writes the whole vault as a Markdown folder: your folders as folders, every note and document as its own file, Bible references as links, plus highlights, bookmarks, reading list and to-dos. Obsidian and other note programs open it directly.
- *Read a Markdown folder …* (*Settings → My data*, also in the Explorer menu) brings a folder of Markdown files into the **active** vault: an Obsidian vault or an earlier export. Each file becomes a **document** (title from the head, the first heading or the file name), folders stay **folders**, `tags:` in the head and `#keywords` in the text become tags, `[text](File.md)` and `[[Folder/Title]]` become links between documents. Files of the export with `verse:` in the head become **notes** of their verse again. **Nothing is replaced:** if a document or note with the same title already exists, both are **merged line by line** (what both have stays once). Whoever goes on writing in Obsidian and reads it back gets no copies. The program makes a **backup** first. Not read are pictures, the lists of the export (marks, reading list, bookmarks, to-dos, start) and folders whose name starts with a dot (.obsidian).
