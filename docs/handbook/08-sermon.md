# 8. The Sermon Editor

Right sidebar → **Sermon** (the group with the pulpit icon). The editor is designed for sermons, Bible studies, devotions, and home groups.

## 8.1 Structure of a sermon

A sermon consists of:

- **Title** (for example "Faithfulness in Exile"),
- **Text** (the Bible passage, for example "Daniel 1:1–21"). With *Current verse as sermon text* you take over the verse you clicked,
- **Core message (one sentence):** What should everyone take away?
- **Outline:** sections (**＋ Add section**), each with
  - Heading,
  - Notes ("Thoughts, explanation, examples …"),
  - **Verses**: *Assign the clicked verse to this passage* or *Quote verse text* (inserts it as a quote into the notes),
  - **Minutes** for this section.

The minutes add up to the **schedule**.

## 8.2 Saving

A sermon is saved as a **normal document** with the tag "Sermon" in the current vault. This makes it searchable, linkable, and part of the Obsidian export. The structure is stored as a comment at the end of the document. With *Open saved sermon …* you bring back an older sermon.

## 8.3 Output

- **Export as handout (HTML):** a printable version that you can print from the browser or save as a PDF.
- **Show as slides (HTML):** the outline as a presentation.
- Print/PDF export of the handout.

## 8.4 From passage to sermon: one way

1. **Read the text** in two or three translations side by side (Parallel).
2. **Observe:** What does it say? Repetitions, connecting words, people, places (Verse overview, Ctrl+G).
3. **Word study** on the two or three most important words (see [Chapter 4](04-original-text-word-study.md)).
4. **Cross-references**: Where does Scripture explain this passage itself?
5. **Formulate the core message in one sentence.**
6. **Draw the outline** from the text itself, not laid over it from outside.
7. **Distribute the time** (minutes per section) and assign verses.
8. **Export the handout** and walk through it aloud once.
